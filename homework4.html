Homework 4 · Stochastic Processes

Penetration Models and Limit Theorems

Modeling system penetration using Bernoulli and Rademacher stochastic processes. Exploring the Law of Large Numbers and Central Limit Theorem through Monte Carlo simulations of n systems under attack.

Summary

This assignment models the penetration process of m attackers targeting n systems using stochastic processes with independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) increments X₁, X₂, ..., representing the penetration score at each step.

We consider two models:

  • Bernoulli model: Xᵢ ∈ {0,1}, with P(Xᵢ = 1) = p (asymmetric, representing successful vs unsuccessful penetration attempts)
  • Rademacher model: Xᵢ ∈ {−1,+1}, with P(Xᵢ = +1) = P(Xᵢ = −1) = 1/2 (symmetric, zero-mean, representing competing attack/defense dynamics)

For increasing values of n, we simulate multiple trajectories and study the distributions of:

  • Total score: Sₙ = ∑ᵢ₌₁ⁿ Xᵢ
  • Average score: Sₙ/n = (1/n)∑ᵢ₌₁ⁿ Xᵢ
  • Normalized total score: Sₙ/√n = (1/√n)∑ᵢ₌₁ⁿ Xᵢ

Stochastic Models

Bernoulli Model

Definition: Xᵢ ∈ {0, 1}

P(Xᵢ = 1) = p, P(Xᵢ = 0) = 1 − p

Mean: E[Xᵢ] = p

Variance: Var(Xᵢ) = p(1−p)

Interpretation: Each step represents an attack attempt. Xᵢ = 1 means successful penetration, Xᵢ = 0 means failure. The parameter p represents the skill level of attackers or vulnerability of systems.

Rademacher Model

Definition: Xᵢ ∈ {−1, +1}

P(Xᵢ = +1) = P(Xᵢ = −1) = 1/2

Mean: E[Xᵢ] = 0

Variance: Var(Xᵢ) = 1

Interpretation: Each step represents a symmetric contest between attack (+1) and defense (−1). The zero mean models a fair game where neither side has systematic advantage. This represents an adversarial security scenario.

Key Properties

  • Independence: Each Xᵢ is independent of others (attacks on different systems are uncorrelated)
  • Identical distribution: All Xᵢ follow the same distribution (systems/attackers are homogeneous)
  • Total score Sₙ: Represents aggregate penetration across n systems
  • Average score Sₙ/n: Represents penetration rate per system
  • Normalized score Sₙ/√n: Properly scaled for CLT convergence

Bernoulli Model Simulation

Simulate penetration attempts with success probability p. As n increases, observe convergence according to the Law of Large Numbers.

Expected Behavior

Law of Large Numbers (LLN)

The average score Sₙ/n converges in probability to the expected value p:

Sₙ/n → p as n → ∞

This means the empirical penetration rate stabilizes around the theoretical probability p for large n.

Total Score Growth

E[Sₙ] = np grows linearly with n

Var(Sₙ) = np(1−p) also grows linearly

SD(Sₙ) = √(np(1−p)) grows as √n

Central Limit Theorem

The standardized total score converges to a normal distribution:

(Sₙ − np) / √(np(1−p)) → N(0,1)

For the normalized score Sₙ/√n:

Sₙ/√n → N(p√n, p(1−p))

The mean grows as √n, variance remains constant.

Rademacher Model Simulation

Simulate symmetric attack/defense dynamics with zero mean. Observe random walk behavior and CLT convergence to standard normal.

Expected Behavior

Law of Large Numbers

The average score converges to the expected value 0:

Sₙ/n → 0 as n → ∞

Despite random fluctuations, the average penetration score approaches zero for large n, reflecting the balanced attack/defense model.

Random Walk Behavior

E[Sₙ] = 0 (zero drift)

Var(Sₙ) = n (variance grows linearly)

SD(Sₙ) = √n (typical deviation grows as √n)

The total score Sₙ performs a symmetric random walk, wandering ±√n around zero.

Central Limit Theorem

The normalized score converges to standard normal:

Sₙ/√n → N(0, 1) as n → ∞

This is the cleanest demonstration of CLT since:

  • Mean is exactly 0 (no centering needed)
  • Variance is exactly 1 (no rescaling needed)
  • Distribution is symmetric

Security Interpretation

In a balanced security scenario, the aggregate score has zero expectation but √n standard deviation. For large networks (n = 10,000), typical scores range from −100 to +100, showing that even in fair contests, randomness creates substantial aggregate variation.

Convergence Study: Varying n

Investigate how convergence behavior changes as the number of systems n increases.

What to Observe

Law of Large Numbers

  • Sₙ/n convergence: As n increases, the distribution of Sₙ/n should become more concentrated around the mean (p for Bernoulli, 0 for Rademacher)
  • Variance decay: Var(Sₙ/n) = Var(Sₙ)/n² decreases as 1/n
  • Practical implication: For large networks, average penetration rate is highly predictable

Central Limit Theorem

  • Shape convergence: Histograms should increasingly resemble bell curves
  • Normalized score: Sₙ/√n should stabilize to a fixed distribution
  • For Rademacher: Should converge to N(0,1)
  • Q-Q plots: Points should align better with theoretical normal as n grows

Security Interpretation

Bernoulli Model: Asymmetric Advantage

When attackers have skill level p > 0.5, they systematically penetrate systems. Key insights:

  • Expected damage: With n systems and probability p, expect np successful penetrations
  • Uncertainty: Standard deviation √(np(1−p)) means actual damage fluctuates around expectation
  • Large networks: Percentage variation decreases as 1/√n, making aggregate outcomes more predictable
  • Example: 10,000 systems with p=0.6 → expect 6,000 ± 49 penetrations (99.7% CI)

Rademacher Model: Balanced Contest

Symmetric attack/defense dynamics with equal capabilities. Key insights:

  • Zero expectation: No systematic advantage, expected total score is 0
  • Random walk: Cumulative score wanders unpredictably, typical magnitude √n
  • Scaling: With 100 systems, typical |Sₙ| ≈ 10; with 10,000 systems, |Sₙ| ≈ 100
  • Defense strategy: Even in balanced scenarios, large networks see substantial aggregate variation

Impact of System Size n and Attackers m

While our model focuses on n steps (systems), we can interpret m attackers as affecting the parameters:

  • More attackers → higher p: In Bernoulli model, more attackers increase success probability
  • Coordinated attacks: Could introduce correlation, breaking i.i.d. assumption
  • Resource constraints: Fixed m attackers spread across growing n systems → decreasing p
  • Detection threshold: Defenders might detect attacks when |Sₙ| exceeds √n significantly

Practical Implications

  • Risk assessment: Use CLT to estimate probability of extreme aggregate penetration scores
  • Capacity planning: LLN ensures that per-system metrics stabilize at scale
  • Anomaly detection: Deviations from √n-scaled expectations may signal coordinated attacks
  • Defense allocation: Focus resources on reducing p rather than just increasing n

Conclusion

This homework demonstrates fundamental limit theorems through a security-motivated lens:

  • Law of Large Numbers: Average penetration rate Sₙ/n converges to expected value, with variance decaying as 1/n
  • Central Limit Theorem: Properly normalized scores Sₙ/√n converge to normal distributions, enabling probabilistic risk assessment
  • Bernoulli vs Rademacher: Asymmetric vs symmetric dynamics lead to fundamentally different behaviors (drift vs random walk)
  • Scaling behavior: As n grows, distributions tighten for Sₙ/n but stabilize for Sₙ/√n

These simulations illustrate how randomness at the individual level (each system/attack) aggregates into predictable patterns at scale, a cornerstone principle in probability theory, statistics, and stochastic modeling.
